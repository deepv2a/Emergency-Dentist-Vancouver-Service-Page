What Is Considered a Dental Emergency?

Our copy

Some dental concerns need attention sooner than others, particularly when you're experiencing significant pain, swelling, or changes to a tooth following an injury.

It's best to contact a dentist if you notice any of the following:

  • Tooth pain: Severe or persistent tooth pain
  • Damaged tooth: A chipped, cracked, or broken tooth
  • Knocked-out tooth: A permanent tooth that has come out
  • Swelling: Swelling around your gums, face, or jaw
  • Infection: Signs of infection, such as an abscess, which is a pocket of pus near the tooth or gum
  • Lost filling or crown: A filling, crown, or other repair that has come out
  • Bleeding: Bleeding from your mouth that doesn't stop
  • Injury: A dental injury from sports or an accident

These symptoms can have different causes, and a dental examination can help identify what's happening. You can also read our guide to what counts as a dental emergency for more detailed advice.

Some symptoms require emergency medical care. Call 911 if you're having difficulty breathing. Visit your nearest emergency department for difficulty swallowing, rapidly spreading swelling around your mouth or neck, significant facial injuries, or heavy bleeding that doesn't stop with firm pressure.

Emergency Dental Treatments We Provide

Our copy

Our emergency dental clinic in Vancouver can help with a range of urgent problems. Your dentist will recommend treatment based on your symptoms and examination findings.

Toothaches & Dental Pain

Tooth pain can have several causes, including decay, cracks, or infection. We'll examine the affected area and recommend treatment based on what we find.

Broken or Chipped Teeth

A damaged tooth may be repaired with bonding, which uses tooth-coloured material to rebuild the tooth, or with a filling or a crown. The appropriate option depends on the extent of the damage.

Knocked-Out Teeth

With a knocked-out permanent tooth, timing matters, because the sooner it's seen, the better the chance of saving it. Contact our office as soon as possible so we can help you arrange care and explain the next steps.

Dental Infections & Abscesses

An infection may cause pain, swelling, or an unpleasant taste in your mouth. Your dentist will assess the area and discuss treatment to address the cause.

Lost Fillings or Crowns

If a filling or crown comes out, the tooth may become sensitive or uncomfortable. We'll examine the area and recommend a suitable repair.

What To Do During a Dental Emergency

Our copy

These steps may help protect your teeth and manage discomfort until you can get dental care. They won't replace an examination or treatment.

For a Knocked-Out Tooth

Pick up a knocked-out permanent tooth by the crown, not the root. If it's safe, gently place it back into its socket without forcing it. Otherwise, keep it moist in milk and seek immediate dental care. Don't reinsert a baby tooth. If you can't reach a dentist, go to an emergency department promptly.

For a Broken Tooth

Gently rinse your mouth with warm water and keep any broken pieces you can find. Avoid chewing on the affected tooth until it's examined.

For Swelling

Apply a cold compress to the outside of your cheek, with a cloth between the pack and your skin. Avoid heat and contact our office promptly. Seek emergency medical care if swelling is spreading rapidly.

For Severe Tooth Pain

Rinsing with warm salt water may help, as can an over-the-counter pain reliever if it's safe for you. Follow the label directions, and don't place aspirin directly against the tooth or gum.

FAQs About Emergency Dentistry

Structure: FAQ trimmed from 4 to 2, removed repeats and the garbled live question

What if my tooth pain comes and goes?

Pain that comes and goes can still indicate an underlying dental concern. It's a good idea to arrange an examination, even if the discomfort improves, so your dentist can check for possible causes.

Can a dental infection go away on its own?

A dental infection generally requires professional treatment. Although the pain may ease temporarily, the underlying cause can remain. It's best to have the tooth examined rather than waiting for the symptoms to return.
